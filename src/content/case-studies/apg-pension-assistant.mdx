## Context

APG is one of the world's largest pension providers, administering over €500 billion for a large
share of the Netherlands under heavy regulation. Written pension communication is hard to follow for
members who are not native Dutch speakers. As APG's own write-up puts it, participants "have
difficulty with the Dutch language" and "are embarrassed about that".

## Business problem

Members needed to understand what a letter says, what the difficult terms mean and what they have to
do, in a language they read comfortably, without a call-centre interaction for every letter. The
tool that came out of it lets a participant scan a letter from Pensioen Schoonmaak and "have it
translated, simplified and read to them".

## My mandate

I designed the solution architecture and the AI processes, wrote the prompts, and was accountable
for design quality and for delivering to the customer's expectations.

## Decisions

- Assemble the first prototype in one day from standard platform components, then dedicate the
  project to system integration and user experience.
- A conversational interface backed by a six-step process (audio transcription, text generation,
  translation, text-to-speech) and a retrieval pipeline that parses incoming letters and pulls
  supporting context from a vector store.
- Twelve two-week sprints with tight coordination with the validation groups, so adoption problems
  surfaced before go-live.

## Delivery

The work ran from 2023 into 2024, twelve iterations from kick-off to the system running in the
on-premises environment. It was developed by Pensioen Schoonmaak together with APG's GroeiFabriek
innovation hub, and unveiled on 8 November 2023 during the Pensioen3daagse event with four languages
— Dutch, Polish, Turkish and English — and the potential to scale to over a hundred.

## Outcome

It started as a proof of concept and went to production. APG's write-up quotes the effect on
participants directly: "the app really does remove barriers. They now understand the point being
made." A Dutch ministry recognised it as one of the best examples of impactful AI integration. It
has since been replaced by another solution — a normal end to a first-generation system, and the
reason this case is written in the past tense.

<blockquote class="case-pull">
  It ran in production under Dutch financial regulation, was recognised by a Dutch ministry as a
  best-practice example of impactful AI integration, and has since been superseded.
</blockquote>

## Reuse

The assistant process pattern, the ingestion and retrieval pipeline and the multilingual output
components became reusable platform capabilities.
